## Elevator Pitch

WattLah is a gamified home energy audit experience that turns a resident’s apartment into an interactive, motivating, and easy-to-understand sustainability dashboard. Instead of presenting dry statistics, we transform energy habits into a visual apartment game where users can walk through a room, scan appliance loads, compare their energy use to a reference level, and preview how small changes could reduce costs and improve their score.

The project is designed to tackle a core sustainability challenge: people often ignore abstract energy data because it feels distant, technical, and disconnected from daily life. WattLah makes sustainability personal, playful, and actionable by showing residents how their everyday habits affect their home, their wallet, and their building community.

---

## About the Project

### Inspiration

Our inspiration came from the idea that sustainability does not fail because people do not care — it fails because the information is not engaging, understandable, or immediately relevant. Traditional energy dashboards often feel like spreadsheets, not experiences. We wanted to build something that turns a familiar home environment into a game-like space that makes energy habits visible and motivating.

The project was inspired by the Ecovolt challenge theme: “Small Green Habits.” We wanted to design a solution that nudges people toward better behavior through simple, understandable feedback rather than guilt, warnings, or complicated data.

### What It Does

WattLah lets users:

- log in with a room number and basic session flow
- explore a pixel-art apartment environment
- scan appliance meters and understand major energy loads
- compare current usage against a reference benchmark
- preview projected energy savings and likely cost reduction
- review a building leaderboard that makes progress feel social and competitive
- track motivated savings habits in a way that feels rewarding instead of punitive

The result is a short, game-like energy audit journey from home entry to savings planning and score comparison.

### Challenges We Ran Into

A few challenges shaped the direction of the project:

- balancing a polished, game-like room interface with a lightweight, no-backend prototype
- making energy data understandable without oversimplifying the problem
- keeping the presentation deterministic and reliable in a web app
- designing a user experience that feels fun, useful, and sustainable without relying on real sensors or complex infrastructure
- making the submission deliverables work within a hackathon timeline while still prioritizing functionality and demo quality

### Accomplishments We’re Proud Of

We are especially proud of:

- building a working end-to-end prototype that feels complete, not just a mockup
- creating an apartment-based interaction model that makes sustainability tangible
- implementing a score and savings preview that turns abstract energy data into something understandable
- designing a responsive, accessible, polished interface with a clear user journey
- delivering a project that is both functional and compelling in a demo setting
- maintaining a strong product story that connects energy habits to measurable behavior change

### How We Built It

We built WattLah using a modern web stack centered around Next.js and React, with TypeScript for type safety and structure. The project uses a top-down pixel-art apartment interface, deterministic mock data, session state for user flow, and a scoring system that compares measured usage to a reference benchmark.

The app is structured around clear product flows:

- login / room selection
- apartment audit / room exploration
- appliance scanning and evaluation
- savings plan preview
- leaderboard and comparison

We intentionally kept the stack lightweight and practical for a hackathon: no backend, no live sensor dependency, and no unnecessary complexity.

### What We Learned

This project reinforced a few important lessons:

- meaningful sustainability tools need to be engaging, not just informative
- clear visual feedback matters more than detailed reporting in motivating action
- small habits become more actionable when they are tied to immediate, personal feedback
- social comparison and visible progress can increase retention and motivation
- a strong prototype must be built around the user journey, not just the technology

### What’s Next

Our next steps would include:

- improving the apartment experience with richer appliance interactions
- adding more detailed savings scenarios and personalized recommendations
- expanding the leaderboard and social motivation loop
- refining the product to better support real-world household energy habits
- testing how users respond to the interface and iterating based on behavioral feedback

## Sustainability

WattLah addresses a real sustainability problem: many people want to reduce energy usage, but they do not know how their choices translate into measurable impact. By turning household energy information into a game-like, personal, and easy-to-understand interface, the project encourages better everyday decisions.

The app promotes small green habits by making energy-awareness more visible and more rewarding. It is designed to help users act on everyday opportunities such as reducing wasteful appliance use, understanding peak loads, and making smarter household energy choices.

This aligns directly with the Ecovolt mission of encouraging sustainable behavior change through practical and engaging experiences rather than passive awareness.

---

## Problem We Are Addressing

The core problem is that sustainability tools often fail because they are too abstract, too technical, or too disconnected from daily life. People are not necessarily unwilling to act; they are simply not seeing a clear link between their habits and real, personal impact.

WattLah tackles this by:

- translating energy consumption into a familiar home context
- making performance visible and relatable
- showing residents how their habits compare to a realistic benchmark
- turning the process into a motivating and interactive experience
- creating a measurable path toward better energy behavior
